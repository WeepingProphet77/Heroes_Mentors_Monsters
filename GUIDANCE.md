## 1. Project Vision

Heroes, Mentors, and Monsters is a church class for older teens and adults, taught together. Its core conviction is simple and unfashionable: **reality exists**, and it has a God-given structure. The scriptures teach us how the world is, and they do it through every means language allows: real historical events, metaphor, poetry, and wisdom literature. All of these genres are telling the truth, each in its own register. Scripture does more than tell us what to do and not do; it reveals the nature of reality itself: our relationship with our Creator, with ourselves, with others, and with the world around us.

Human beings, made in the image of a speaking God, tell stories that express our understanding of that reality. Most stories fade. But the ones that capture the most real elements of reality persist and endure; they survive precisely because they are onto something true. This is why the same archetypes recur in Star Wars, The Lord of the Rings, The Chronicles of Narnia, Harry Potter, Pinocchio, The Legend of Zelda, the Grimm fairy tales, and myths across cultures. These stories resonate because they touch patterns so real that we all inherently recognize them. The claim of the class, following C.S. Lewis and J.R.R. Tolkien, is that these enduring stories are echoes of the one True Story. The patterns point beyond themselves, and in the gospel the myth became fact.

This conviction is developed with help from two main guides. Jonathan Pageau and The Symbolic World supply the account of reality's symbolic structure. Jordan B. Peterson supplies the account of why the human soul navigates by these stories.

From Dr. Jordan B. Peterson, particularly *Maps of Meaning: The Architecture of Belief*: Peterson demonstrates that these archetypal stories are not decoration but load-bearing: they are the maps by which human beings have always navigated between order and chaos, the known and the unknown. The hero who voluntarily confronts the dragon and returns with the treasure is not merely a plot; it is a description of how a human life is supposed to work. Where Pageau shows us the symbolic structure of reality, Peterson shows us that the human soul is built to run on that structure. The class draws on both, and then goes one step further than Peterson does: the map is accurate because there is a Mapmaker, and in Christ the map and the territory meet.

### The Logos: the keystone idea

The idea that holds the whole class together is the **Logos** of John 1:1-5 (NIV):

> "In the beginning was the Word, and the Word was with God, and the Word was God. He was with God in the beginning. Through him all things were made; without him nothing was made that has been made. In him was life, and that life was the light of all mankind. The light shines in the darkness, and the darkness has not overcome it."

The Greek word John chose, *Logos*, meant more than "word." To the Greek mind it was the rational ordering principle of the cosmos, the reason the world hangs together and makes sense. John's staggering claim is that this ordering principle is not an abstraction or a force. It is a Person, he was in the beginning with God, he *is* God, and he took on flesh and dwelt among us.

This is the keystone that joins everything the class draws on. If Pageau is right that reality has a symbolic structure, and Peterson is right that our souls are built to navigate by story, the Logos is why: all things were made *through him*, so his pattern is stamped on everything, including us. The structure of reality is personal. Every session should stand on this, and the light-in-darkness image of John 1:5 is the visual and thematic signature of the whole course.

**The one-sentence pitch:** The stories we love keep telling us the same thing because it is true, and Scripture tells it first and best.

### What success looks like
- A teenager leaves seeing that the movies they love are borrowing from Scripture, not the other way around, and can name the hero, the mentor, and the monster in a story they love and find them in a Bible story.
- An adult leaves with a richer way to read the Bible: not just as rules or history, but as the revealed structure of reality.
- Everyone leaves seeing themselves inside God's story, with Christ as the true Hero.

## 2. Theological Framing and Guardrails

This class is taught in a **Protestant/Evangelical** context. Pageau is Eastern Orthodox; we use his insights about symbolism and the structure of reality gratefully, but the class stands on these commitments:

1. **Scripture is the final authority.** Symbolism serves the text; it never overrides or replaces it. Every symbolic reading must be defensible from the Bible itself.
2. **Christ is the center.** Every archetype finds its fulfillment in Jesus. He is the true Hero who slays the dragon, the true Mentor who sends the Helper, the one who faces the true Monster (sin, death, the serpent) and wins. Every session lands here.
3. **The parallels make Scripture more real, not less.** This is a load-bearing conviction and every contributor must get it right. Archetypes appear in myths older than the biblical texts and in stories written last year. This does **not** mean Scripture is derivative fiction assembled from earlier tales. The logic runs the other way: an echo does not create the sound. God authored reality before any myth was told, so the pattern was true first; the older myths anticipate it, the later stories keep rediscovering it, and Scripture is where the pattern touched down in history. The biblical events are to be taught as **actual events**: real people, real places, datable, witnessed. So the class never says "the Bible is like the myths." It says: everywhere else the pattern is a rumor; here it has an address. The recurrence before and after is evidence that these truths are woven into the fabric of reality, which makes Scripture not one more story but the hinge where the story became fact.

   **On the word "myth" itself.** Lewis used it in the literary sense: a story of enormous shape and power, truth-value not specified. Ordinary English now uses it to mean a falsehood, so "true myth" can land on a listener as a polite concession that the gospel is fiction. Lewis is a friendly voice and "myth became fact" stays in the course, but the word is never projected on a slide, printed on a tile, or spoken to the room without its definition attached first. Where a slide must carry the idea alone, lead with the fact (that it happened) and let Lewis support it, rather than leading with the category. The teacher notes carry the full quotation and the framing; the wall carries the claim.
4. **Pattern, not allegory-hunting.** We teach that Scripture reveals real patterns woven into creation. We do not force secret meanings onto every detail, and we distinguish clearly between what Scripture teaches and what a story merely illustrates.
5. **Stories are illustrations, not authorities.** Star Wars and Harry Potter are useful because they borrow the pattern. They are never treated as inspired, and their flaws and worldview gaps can be named honestly.
6. **Avoid tradition-specific practices** that would be foreign to the audience: veneration of icons, prayers to saints, and similar. Patristic and Orthodox sources may be quoted where they illuminate, the same way we would quote Lewis or Augustine.
7. **Handle magic in fiction pastorally.** Some families are cautious about Harry Potter and fairy tale magic. The teacher notes must include a brief, respectful treatment: the difference between literary magic as story mechanics and the occult practices Scripture forbids. Never mock the concern.
8. **Use Peterson's psychology as a witness, not a foundation.** Maps of Meaning establishes brilliantly that the archetypal patterns are real, universal, and indispensable to human life. But Peterson's account is psychological and stops short of the stories being true in history. The class affirms his observations and then presses through them: the patterns are written into us because the God of Scripture wrote them into reality, and the Hero's story actually happened, in Judea, in the flesh. Peterson material should be presented as "even viewed purely psychologically, the pattern holds," never as the ceiling of the claim. Where his framing diverges from orthodox Christian teaching (e.g., treating the resurrection primarily as archetype), the teacher notes must name the divergence plainly and kindly.
9. **Default Bible translation: NIV**, chosen for accessibility. Quote it accurately. ESV or KJV may be used where a specific rendering helps, with the translation noted.
10. **Introduce the guides once, then use their tools.** Pageau and Peterson are introduced to the class by name exactly once, on session 2 slide 12, where each is given an occupation, a job in the course, and the rule that governs every later mention: neither man is preaching, and that is precisely why his agreement counts. Session 1 states their two ideas without attaching the names, so the class weighs the ideas before it is told whose they are. After session 2, a surname may appear bare.

    Two rules follow. First, **every later mention must be doing one of two jobs**: corroborating the claim from outside the faith, or handing the teacher a lens that makes something visible. A name that is not doing one of those is name-dropping; drop the name and keep the idea. Second, **do not let Peterson become a foil.** The formula "Peterson says X, true as far as it goes, but" is corrosive if it runs every week: it trains the room to hear his name as the setup for a correction, and an outside witness who is always slightly wrong is worth nothing as corroboration. Press past him only where the divergence genuinely matters (sessions 2, 6, and 8). Elsewhere let him be simply right, and say so. Session 7 says so explicitly, on purpose.

    **Pageau's tradition is stated, not hidden.** The class is told he is Eastern Orthodox, on the slide and in the notes. Discovering it later, unprompted, reads as concealment; naming it in advance models charitable reading across traditions. Guardrail 6 still governs: his tradition's distinctive practices are not imported.

    **Peterson's personal faith is not the course's business and not its foundation.** The teacher notes answer the question honestly if it is asked, without claiming him and without dismissing him. Do not write a dated claim about his religious position into any deliverable; the class does not rest on it either way, and if it did he would not be worth citing.

Key friendly voices to draw on: C.S. Lewis ("myth became fact," Narnia), J.R.R. Tolkien ("On Fairy-Stories," eucatastrophe), G.K. Chesterton (Orthodoxy, "The Ethics of Elfland"), Jonathan Pageau (The Symbolic World, symbolism of chaos and order, the pattern of heaven and earth), Jordan B. Peterson (Maps of Meaning, the biblical lecture series; order and chaos, the known and the unknown, voluntary confrontation with the dragon, the redemptive power of truthful speech, sacrifice as the discovery that the future can be bargained with).

## 3. Audience: Older Teens and Adults

Every session must land for both a 16-year-old and a 50-year-old, taught together. The audience can handle real depth; the risk is not going over anyone's head but losing the room in abstraction. The method is **story first, theory second**: every abstract idea is introduced through a concrete story moment before it is named.

Session arc (this shapes both slides and notes):

- **The Hook:** Open with a story moment with immediate pull. A scene description, an image, a short retelling. Earn attention before asking for thought.
- **The Pattern:** Name the archetype in one memorable sentence anyone can repeat a week later.
- **The Scripture:** Find the pattern in the Bible and show it was there first. This is the center of gravity of the session.
- **The Depth:** The Pageau/Lewis/Tolkien material: symbolism, the structure of reality, true myth. With this audience it can run 10 to 15 minutes and carry real intellectual weight. Teens are often the most hungry for exactly this; do not water it down for them.
- **The Landing:** Christ, and us. Where is this pattern in your life this week?

Discussion questions come in two tiers in the teacher notes: **Starter** (accessible, story-level, good for warming up a quiet room or drawing out teens) and **Deeper** (theological and personal). The teacher chooses in the moment.

Session length assumption: **45 to 60 minutes**. Presentation should carry about 25 to 30 minutes; discussion fills the rest.

## 4. Course Structure: 11 Sessions

Eleven sessions, with a built-in flex: sessions can compress to 9 or expand to 12 (options noted below). One archetype or pattern per week, one anchor Scripture, two to three anchor stories.

| # | Session Title | Core Pattern | Anchor Scripture | Anchor Stories |
|---|---------------|--------------|------------------|----------------|
| 1 | The World Has a Shape | Reality has a God-given structure; the Logos through whom all things were made; order and chaos | Genesis 1; John 1:1-5 | Creation vs. chaos across myths; opening scenes of beloved stories |
| 2 | Why Stories Ring True | Archetypes; myth became fact | Ecclesiastes 3:11; Acts 17:22-31 | Tolkien and Lewis's conversation; patterns shared by Star Wars, LOTR, fairy tales |
| 3 | The Call of the Hero | Leaving home; reluctance; being chosen | Genesis 12:1-4 (Abram); Exodus 3 (Moses); 1 Samuel 16 (David) | Luke Skywalker, Frodo, Harry's letter |
| 4 | The Mentor | Wisdom passed down; the guide who cannot walk the road for you | 1 Samuel 3 (Eli/Samuel); 2 Timothy 1 (Paul/Timothy) | Obi-Wan, Gandalf, Dumbledore, Jiminy Cricket, Zelda's old man in the cave ("It's dangerous to go alone! Take this.") |
| 5 | The Monster Outside | Chaos, dragons, giants; evil confronted | 1 Samuel 17 (Goliath); Job 41 (Leviathan) | Smaug, the Grimm dragons, Vader as the looming threat |
| 6 | The Monster Within | Sin crouching at the door; temptation and the divided heart | Genesis 4:7; Romans 7 | Gollum, Anakin's fall, Pinocchio's lies, the wolf in Grandma's clothing |
| 7 | Into the Belly of the Beast | Death and resurrection; descent before rising | Jonah 1-2; Matthew 12:39-40 | Pinocchio in the whale, Gandalf's fall and return |
| 8 | The Life Laid Down | Voluntary sacrifice and redemption; the life no one takes, but which is laid down freely | Isaiah 53; John 10:11-18; John 15:13 | Aslan at the Stone Table, Obi-Wan on the Death Star, Harry walking into the forest, Vader redeemed by Luke's willingness to die |
| 9 | The Least Likely One | The fool, the youngest son, the overlooked | 1 Samuel 16:7; 1 Corinthians 1:26-29 | Grimm's youngest brothers, Cinderella, Sam Gamgee, a boy in green from a small village, shepherd David |
| 10 | The Return of the King | Kingship, restoration, the world set right | Isaiah 9:6-7; Revelation 19-21 | Aragorn's return, the fall of the Empire, "everything sad coming untrue" |
| 11 | Living Inside the Story | We are in the story now; Christ the true Hero, the Spirit our guide | Hebrews 12:1-2; Ephesians 2:10 | The class's own stories; every thread tied to Christ |

Notes on sessions 7 and 8: they are deliberately adjacent and distinct. Session 7 is the **pattern** of descent and return (down into the belly, up transformed). Session 8 is the **choice** at the heart of it: the hero who could walk away and instead lays down his life for others, and the redemption that flows from it. Session 8 sits closest to the gospel of any session and should be treated as the summit of the course's middle act. John 10:18 ("No one takes it from me, but I lay it down of my own accord," NIV) is its keystone verse, and the Peterson emphasis on sacrifice as the deepest discovery of story belongs here.

**Compression to 9:** merge 5+6 (The Monster, outside and within); fold 2 into 1.
**Expansion to 12:** split session 2 into "Why Stories Ring True" and "True Myth: Tolkien, Lewis, and the Gospel"; or add a session on "The Wasteland and the Garden" (exile and homecoming; Eden, Babylon, the Shire).

Session numbering is fixed at the 11-session scheme for all filenames and image names, even if taught in more or fewer weeks.

## 5. Deliverables

### 5.1 The Presentation (single HTML experience)

- **One file: `index.html`**, containing the entire course as a single rich, browser-based experience. Not a folder of separate decks; one continuous, beautifully navigable whole.
- **Integrated navigation, all inside the HTML:**
  - A **course home screen**: series title art, the one-sentence pitch, and a visual menu of all eleven sessions (think chapter-select screen, each session with its own artwork tile and title).
  - Each session opens as its own **slide sequence** within the experience: full-viewport slides advanced by arrow keys, spacebar, click/tap, and on-screen next/back controls.
  - A persistent, unobtrusive way back to the home screen from any slide (small menu button or Escape key).
  - A subtle progress indicator showing position within the current session.
  - **URL hash routing** (e.g. `index.html#s03/5` for session 3, slide 5) so any slide is bookmarkable and the teacher can resume mid-course after a refresh without clicking through.
- **Self-contained and offline-capable.** All CSS and JavaScript inline in the file. No CDN links, no external fonts required at runtime (system font stack with a serif display fallback is fine). The class may be taught in a room with no internet.
- **Images via relative links only**, all pointing into the `images/` folder: `<img src="images/s03-heros-call-hero.jpg">`. Never absolute paths, never remote URLs.
- **Built for incremental growth.** Sessions are added to the file batch by batch as they are approved. Unbuilt sessions appear on the home screen as visible but clearly marked "coming" tiles, so the experience is always presentable.
- **Visually rich but readable at distance.** Large type (minimum roughly 28px equivalent for body text on slides), high contrast, generous space. Designed for a 16:9 projector but responsive enough to review on a laptop. Images do the atmospheric work; text stays minimal. A slide should never be a paragraph.
- **Standard slide flow per session:** title slide, hook, the pattern (one memorable sentence), story exploration (2 to 4 slides), Scripture (text on screen, readable), go deeper, the landing (Christ and us), discussion slide with the week's key question.
- **Graceful missing images.** Every `<img>` gets meaningful `alt` text and the layout must not break if an image file is not yet present. This is essential because ChatGPT adds images after Claude builds structure.
- **Maintainability rule:** because many contributions land in one file, the internal structure must be strictly sectioned and commented (one clearly delimited block per session) so any contributor can find and edit a session without touching the rest.

### 5.2 Teacher Notes (Word)

- **One .docx per session**, named `session-01-notes.docx` etc., in the `notes/` folder.
- **Structure of each notes file:**
  1. Session title, week number, core pattern in one sentence
  2. Teacher preparation (what to read, watch, or gather; roughly 30 minutes of prep)
  3. Session goals (one for teens, one for adults, one shared)
  4. Timeline with minute marks matching the slide flow
  5. Talking points keyed to slide numbers (guidance, not a word-for-word script)
  6. Scripture texts printed in full (NIV)
  7. Discussion questions in two tiers (Starter / Deeper)
  8. Go Deeper notes: the Pageau/Lewis/Tolkien material with citations, so a teacher can answer follow-ups
  9. Pastoral notes where needed (e.g., the magic-in-fiction conversation in the Harry Potter sessions)
  10. Take-home line: the one sentence families should carry out the door
- Tone: warm, confident, practical. Written for a volunteer teacher who loves the Lord and the stories but has not read Pageau.

### 5.3 Optional Supporting Files

- `handouts/` for any printable family take-home pages (PDF), only if requested.
- `README.md` briefly explaining the folder for anyone who stumbles into it.

## 6. Folder and File Conventions

```
Heroes, Mentors, and Monsters/
├── GUIDANCE.md            (this document; the constitution)
├── README.md              (short orientation)
├── index.html             (the entire course experience, single file)
├── images/                (ALL images live here, flat, no subfolders)
├── notes/
│   └── session-01-notes.docx ... session-11-notes.docx
└── handouts/              (optional)
```

**Image naming convention:** `s{NN}-{slug}-{role}.jpg` (or .png where transparency matters).
- `NN` = two-digit session number; use `s00` for course-wide art (index page, series title art).
- `slug` = short kebab-case description.
- `role` = one of: `hero` (main title/atmosphere image for a slide), `illus` (supporting illustration), `tile` (session artwork on the home screen menu), `icon` (small decorative element).
- Examples: `s00-series-title-hero.jpg`, `s05-dragon-hoard-hero.jpg`, `s07-belly-of-whale-illus.jpg`.

Claude writes the `<img>` tags with these exact filenames and descriptive alt text **before** the images exist. This filename-first workflow is the contract between the two AIs. A running image manifest lives at `images/MANIFEST.md`: every needed image, its filename, its slide, and a full art direction prompt. ChatGPT works from the manifest.

- Does not update checkboxes, the outstanding-requests block, deviation notes, counts, GUIDANCE.md, README.md, or anything in git. Bookkeeping that looks helpful still has to be verified and redone, so it costs time rather than saving it.

**Jason (owner and editor):**
- Final say on all theology, tone, and content
- Reviews each session before it is considered done
- Ferries context between the AIs when needed (e.g., pasting manifest entries into ChatGPT)

**Why the lane is drawn there.** Two agents editing the same bookkeeping produces drift that is expensive to catch: a count updated in one file and not another, a checkbox flipped while the README still calls the image outstanding, a status line that contradicts the manifest. One agent owning the record end to end is what keeps these four files telling the same story.

**Rule for both AIs:** never rename existing files, never restructure folders, and never edit the other's domain without flagging it. Additive contributions are safe; destructive ones require Jason's approval.

## 8. Visual and Verbal Style

### Art direction (for all images)
- **Style:** painterly, storybook-illustration feel; think classic book plates and golden-age illustration rather than photorealism or comic style. Warm light against deep shadow; the visual language of light overcoming darkness.
- **Palette:** deep blues and near-blacks for chaos, monsters, and night; warm golds, ambers, and firelight for wisdom, home, and revelation; muted greens for journeys and growth. Consistent across all sessions.
- **Copyright rule: recognizable, not infringing.** The goal is the flash of recognition without legal exposure. The audience should think "that's clearly the Star Wars story" while the image itself remains an archetypal figure. The technique is **faceless figures plus unmistakable context**:
  - Figures are faceless, silhouetted, hooded, or seen from behind. No actor likenesses, ever.
  - Recognition comes from archetypal cues and environment, not from copying designs. A farm boy gazing at a twin-sunned desert horizon; an old robed mentor with a glowing blade; a towering black-caped figure, masked and faceless, in a cold corridor. A grey wandering wizard with staff and pointed hat; small barefoot travelers in a green land; a burning eye above a wasteland; a crown returning to a throne.
  - The same approach covers every franchise: a boy with round glasses seen from behind facing a serpent; a wooden boy whose nose betrays him; a girl in a red hood in a dark wood.
  - Hard limits: no logos or title treatments, no exact prop or costume replicas (silhouette and suggestion, not blueprint accuracy), no shot-for-shot recreations of movie frames, no trademarked names inside image files.
  - Alt text and the manifest describe images archetypally ("the masked dark lord," "the grey mentor"), not by trademarked character names.
  - Ancient and public-domain material (Grimm, myth, Scripture scenes) carries no such restrictions; render it freely.
  - **This rule governs imagery only.** Written and spoken content (slide text, teacher notes, discussion questions) refers to characters and stories by name: Luke Skywalker, Gandalf, Darth Vader, Harry Potter. Naming and discussing them is the point of the class and is ordinary commentary. The images stay archetypal; the words stay specific.
- **No text baked into images.** All words live in HTML so they stay editable.
- **Content standards:** monsters may be genuinely fearsome and darkness genuinely dark; this audience can handle weight, and the light means more against it. Still never gory or grotesque; this hangs on a church wall, so to speak.

### Writing voice
- Clear, warm, and a little wonder-struck. The subject is enchantment; the prose should not be flat.
- Short sentences on slides. Full sentences, no fragments, in teacher notes.
- **No em dashes anywhere in any deliverable.** Use commas, colons, or parentheses.
- Spell the series title as **Heroes, Mentors, and Monsters** in all displayed content, matching the folder name.
- Scripture quotations exact, with book, chapter, verse, and translation noted.
- Humor is welcome and should be gentle; never sarcastic about Scripture or about anyone's favorite story.

## 9. Working Process

### Current status: revision, not construction

**The build is complete.** All eleven sessions exist inside `index.html` (137 slides as of this revision), all eleven teacher notes documents are written, and all 71 images in the manifest have been generated and placed. Nothing in the course is a stub.

What this changes for contributors: assume a session already exists before you write anything. Read the existing slides and the matching notes document for a session before proposing a change to it, and prefer surgical edits to rewrites. The build process below is retained because it still governs any new session added under the expansion options in section 4.

### The build process (used for sessions 1 to 11)

1. **Foundation first:** Claude builds the index.html shell (home screen, navigation, visual system) with session 1 complete inside it, plus session 1 notes and manifest entries. Jason reviews. That approved session becomes the pattern for all others.
2. **Then batches:** sessions produced in small batches (2 to 3 at a time), each reviewed before the next batch.
3. **Images in parallel:** once a session's manifest entries exist, ChatGPT can generate its images at any time. HTML must always render acceptably without them.

### The revision process (current)

4. **Living documents:** this document, the slides, and the notes are edited in place. No formal version numbers, but material changes get a dated entry in the Revision Log (section 11).
5. **Definition of done (per session):** slides complete and reviewed, notes complete and reviewed, all manifest images present and loading, Jason has taught it to himself once without confusion.
6. **Slides and notes move together.** The teacher notes key their talking points and their minute-by-minute timeline to slide numbers. Any slide inserted, removed, or reordered requires the matching notes document to be renumbered in the same piece of work, and the timeline re-budgeted to absorb the change. A slide edit that leaves the notes pointing at the wrong slide is not finished, it is broken.
7. **Check the blast radius of a phrase.** Signature lines recur across the slides, the home screen tile subtitles, the image manifest, the notes, and this document. Before rewording one, search for it everywhere. A phrase corrected on its own slide but left standing on the home screen, where no teacher is present to frame it, is worse than not having touched it at all.
8. **Preserve the .docx structure.** The notes documents follow the ten-part structure in section 5.2. Edit them without flattening that structure, without losing the Georgia body font and the gold timeline marks, and without disturbing the heading styles.
